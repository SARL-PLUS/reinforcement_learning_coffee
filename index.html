About

Half-baked ideas welcome

The Reinforcement Learning Coffee meets on the first Friday of every month. The entry level is intentionally low and everyone is welcome, from newcomers to experienced practitioners. A session can be an initial idea or open problem, a more involved project, your latest paper or a paper idea, or simply working through a recent paper together.

The format grew out of the Machine Learning Coffee at CERN, where machine learning and reinforcement learning were introduced to the CERN accelerator complex. Talks since then have ranged from quantum optimal control and laser alignment to heat pumps, air traffic and chemical oscillators.

  1. Low barrier

    No polished results needed. Open questions and failed experiments make good sessions.

  2. Real systems

    A soft spot for RL on physical systems: accelerators, lasers, mechatronics, processes.

  3. Across institutions

    Speakers from universities, research institutes and industry, in Salzburg and remote.

Format

One hour, shaped by who shows up

Every session runs 60 minutes on Teams. The host picks one of three formats at the start, depending on how many people are in the call.

  1. 2 to 5 people

    Round table

    • 0–10 Short round: name, what you work on, one open question
    • 10–30 Speaker walks through the work, slides optional, interrupt anytime
    • 30–55 Open discussion, everyone on camera if possible
    • 55–60 Next session and who presents

  2. 6 to 15 people

    Talk and discussion

    • 0–5 Welcome, news from the community
    • 5–35 Talk, about 25 minutes, clarifying questions in between
    • 35–55 Discussion led by the host, raise hand or unmute
    • 55–60 Next session and call for talks

  3. 16 people or more

    Seminar

    • 0–5 Welcome, microphones off by default
    • 5–40 Talk, about 30 minutes, questions go into the chat
    • 40–55 Host reads out the chat questions, then raised hands
    • 55–60 Next session; slides are linked in the archive if the speaker agrees

Recordings only with the speaker's consent. Early ideas and unfinished work stay in the room.
